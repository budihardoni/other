Step 1. Pick a direction people can recognize

When someone lands on your profile, make it easy for them to understand what they’ll get if they follow you.

Start with three questions:

  • What do you enjoy creating?
  • What do you already know or feel excited to learn?
  • What would you happily make more than once?

For me, things started clicking when I moved into creative AI videos. That gave me a direction, with plenty of room to try different ideas.

And that’s the part people miss:

you can stick to one direction while experimenting with how you present it

Within AI content, you could try cinematic stories, funny POV videos, transformations, or simple tutorials. You don’t have to decide your entire creative identity before posting anything.

Then choose three formats you want to test. Write down two video ideas for each. You now have six starting points.

Step 2. Build an idea bank you’ll actually use

Let me be real with you: I don’t wake up with a genius idea every morning.

That’s why I keep an idea bank.

Create a saved folder on Instagram, Pinterest, or YouTube.

Whenever something makes you stop scrolling, save it—and write down what caught your attention.

  • Was it the first shot?
  • The surprise at the end?
  • Something funny?
  • Something you immediately wanted to send to a friend?

“This got a lot of views” is a starting point. Try to go one step further.

I also look for videos that performed unusually well for the person who posted them.

For example, if someone usually gets around 10,000 views and one video gets 200,000, that’s worth looking at.

A huge view count gives you something to investigate. It doesn’t guarantee the same result on your account.

Step 3. Turn a reference into your own idea

You found something you love. Now ask:

“What would my version of this look like?”

The useful part of that example is the creative process: I found something that excited me and gave it a new setting, a new execution, and my own presence.

You can borrow inspiration from a visual hook, a story structure, a mood, or an unexpected reveal. Then build your own video around it.

When a specific creator’s idea strongly inspires yours, credit them. Give people a way to discover the person who inspired you.

🧚🏻‍♀️ Your turn: Pick one saved reference and complete these three lines:

  • What I love about it: __________
  • What I’ll change: __________
  • What makes my version personal: __________

If the last line is hard to answer, spend a little more time developing the idea.

Step 4. Give yourself room to experiment

My biggest early mistake was forcing talking-head videos because I thought that was what a “real creator” was supposed to do.

I spent months trying to make that format work. Testing creative AI videos helped me find something that fit me much better.

So give yourself permission to try things.

  • Use the six ideas you wrote down earlier as your first small batch
  • Choose a posting pace you can realistically keep up with
  • And give each format more than one attempt.

Before making each video, write down one thing you want to learn:

“Will showing the transformation immediately make the opening more interesting?”

Or:

“Will a personal story make people more interested in this idea?”

That gives you something useful to look at afterward.

🧚🏻‍♀️ Your turn:

  • Choose your next three videos
  • Put a realistic publishing date beside each one.

You don’t need to feel completely ready. You need something you can publish and learn from.

Step 5. Use Trial Reels to explore new ideas

Trial Reels became a big part of my process.

Instagram shows them to people who don’t follow you first, which makes them useful for trying new formats and ideas.

Some followers can still come across them through shares or other places.

Initial performance metrics become available after approximately 24 hours.

Instagram explains the feature here.

If you have the Trial option when publishing, use it to test a clear idea.

Step 6. Give people a reason to follow

A video can get someone’s attention. Your account needs to give them a reason to stay.

Look at your profile from the perspective of someone discovering you today.

  • Can they tell what you create?
  • Do your pinned posts show your strongest work?
  • Is there more of the thing that made them interested in the first place?

Think about what viewers can look forward to: another cinematic story, another transformation, another funny POV, or a breakdown of how you made something.

Make sure the promise matches what you actually want to keep creating.

🧚🏻‍♀️ Your turn:

  • Update your bio
  • Choose three posts that best introduce your work.

Step 7. Look at the results - and use them

Once you’ve published a batch of videos, take a moment to look back.

Check views alongside watch time, shares, saves, and the followers each video brought in, wherever those insights are available.

Each number helps you ask a different question.

  • Did people keep watching?
  • Did they want to share it?
  • Did they care enough to follow for more?

When something performs well, write down your best explanation. Then test that explanation in a new video. Keep the part you think worked and give people a fresh idea.
